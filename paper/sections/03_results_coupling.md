## 3. Results

### 3.1 How the physics varies with the coupling constant

The coupling constant compares the van der Waals interaction with the drive: $V/\Omega=(R_b/r)^6$ for two atoms a distance $r$ apart, and $g=V_0/\Omega=(R_b/a)^6$ for a chain with spacing $a$ and nearest-neighbour interaction $V_0=C_6/a^6$. The earlier calculations in `study/` (full Hilbert space, all pair interactions, $\hbar=\Omega=a=1$; dynamics at $\Delta=0$ from $|g\cdots g\rangle$) reproduce established physics; here they validate the solvers and separate the effects of $g$ from boundary effects.

**Two-atom blockade and the Qiskit baseline.** Without interaction the two atoms Rabi-oscillate independently and reach $P_{11}=1$ at $\Omega t=\pi$ in a product state (Fig. 2). At $V/\Omega=20$ the doubly excited state is shifted out of resonance, and the pair oscillates between $|gg\rangle$ and $|W\rangle=(|gr\rangle+|rg\rangle)/\sqrt2$ at the collective frequency $\sqrt2\,\Omega$: the two-atom blockade observed in Refs. [2,3]. The mean excitation number follows $\sin^2(\sqrt2\,\Omega t/2)$ to within $1.7\times10^{-3}$<!-- source: study/results/qiskit_baseline.csv, rows V_over_omega = 20; max |mean_excitations - sin^2(sqrt(2) omega_t / 2)| = 1.66e-3 over 161 times (computed 27 Sept) -->. The entanglement entropy of one atom reaches 0.9994 bits at $\Omega t\approx\pi/\sqrt2$<!-- source: study/results/qiskit_baseline.csv, V_over_omega = 20, max entropy_bits = 0.99939 at omega_t = 2.238 (computed 27 Sept) -->, where the state is close to the entangled $|W\rangle$ state [4]. Qiskit and SciPy evolutions agree to $1.0\times10^{-14}$ in the state vector<!-- source: study/results/summary.json, checks.qiskit_scipy_state_error_V0 = 1.02e-14 and checks.qiskit_scipy_state_error_V20 = 5.2e-15 -->.

**Blockade versus coupling strength.** Averaged over $0\le\Omega t\le2\pi$, the double-excitation probability falls from the free-atom value $3/8$ to $9.8\times10^{-3}$ at $V/\Omega=5$ and $1.5\times10^{-4}$ at $V/\Omega=40$ (Fig. 3)<!-- source: study/results/blockade_scan.csv, time_mean_P11 = 0.37500 (V = 0), 0.009771 (V = 5), 0.00014730 (V = 40) -->. Adiabatic elimination of $|rr\rangle$, which couples to $|W\rangle$ with matrix element $\Omega/\sqrt2$, gives for $V\gg\Omega$

$$
P_{11}(t)\simeq\frac{\Omega^2}{2V^2}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\sqrt2\,\Omega t}{2}\right),\qquad
\overline{P_{11}}\simeq0.2356\left(\frac{\Omega}{V}\right)^2, \qquad (3.1)
$$

where the bar denotes the same time average. The computed $(V/\Omega)^2\,\overline{P_{11}}$ is 0.2376, 0.2361 and 0.2357 at $V/\Omega=10$, 20 and 40<!-- source: study/results/blockade_scan.csv, (V_over_omega)^2 * time_mean_P11 (computed 27 Sept). The prefactor in Eq. (3.1) is (1/2)[1/2 - sin(2 sqrt(2) pi)/(4 sqrt(2) pi)] = 0.23556, i.e. 1/2 times the window average of sin^2(sqrt(2) t / 2) -->, converging to Eq. (3.1): the blockade is strong but never complete. This residual double occupancy is why we keep the full interaction and check the restricted bases of some pilot calculations against the full Hilbert space (P1).

**Interaction switch-off controls.** Figure 4 compares a six-atom chain at $V_0/\Omega=5$ ($R_b/a=1.31$) with two idealized controls: all interactions of atom 2 removed with its drive kept, and all interactions across the central cut removed. The decoupled atom 2 has excitation probability $\sin^2(\Omega t/2)$ to within $1.2\times10^{-15}$ and stays unentangled<!-- source: study/results/summary.json, checks.isolated_atom_rabi_error = 1.2e-15 and checks.isolated_atom_max_entropy = 6.4e-16 bits -->. With the cut severed, the half-chain entropy stays below $2\times10^{-16}$ bits<!-- source: study/results/summary.json, checks.disconnected_halves_max_entropy = 1.6e-16 -->, compared with 1.25 bits at $\Omega t=4$ in the intact chain<!-- source: study/results/defect_intact.csv, S_012_bits = 1.2497 at omega_t = 4.0 (maximum of the window) -->. With atom 2 decoupled, the half-chain entropy still reaches 0.017 bits by $\Omega t=4$<!-- source: study/results/defect_atom_2_isolated.csv, max S_012_bits = 0.0171 at omega_t = 4.0, the end of the window (computed 27 Sept) -->, mainly because atoms 1 and 3 remain coupled through the tail $V_0/2^6=0.078\,\Omega$<!-- source: V0/2^6 = 5/64 = 0.078 (pair 1-3); V0/3^6 = 5/729 = 0.0069 for the next pairs across the cut (0-3 and 1-4), from interactions(chain(6), 5, isolated=2) in study/run_august_experiments.py -->. Weak tails therefore matter for the interaction cutoffs of Sec. 3.3.

**Geometry.** Four atoms with $V_0/\Omega=5$ at distance $a$ form a chain, a square of side $a$ or an $a\times2a$ rectangle (Fig. 5). Entanglement follows the short, strongly blockaded bonds. In the rectangle, the bipartition that cuts both short bonds reaches 1.97 bits at $\Omega t=2.23$, close to the two-bit value for two independent blockaded pairs passing through $|W\rangle$; the bipartition that cuts no short bond stays below 0.031 bits<!-- source: study/results/geometry_rectangle.csv, max S_02_bits = 1.9655 at omega_t = 2.225; max S_01_bits = 0.0304 (computed 27 Sept) -->. In the chain, the interleaved bipartition $\{0,2\}|\{1,3\}$, which cuts all three nearest-neighbour bonds, carries 1.52 bits at $\Omega t=2$, twice the contiguous value of 0.75 bits<!-- source: study/results/summary.json, geometry[Chain]: S_02_at_t2 = 1.5157, S_01_at_t2 = 0.7505 (also study/results/geometry_chain.csv at omega_t = 2.0) -->. A chain with alternating short and long gaps, the subject of Sec. 3.2, is the one-dimensional analogue: strongly blockaded pairs, weakly coupled.

**Finite-chain ground state.** Figure 6 maps the ground state of an eight-atom open chain for $-3\le\Delta/\Omega\le7$ and $0.5\le g\le12$, that is, $0.89\le R_b/a\le1.51$. For $g\ge8$ and $\Delta/\Omega\ge3$ the density lies between 0.48 and 0.53 and the gap between $0.098\,\Omega$ and $0.165\,\Omega$<!-- source: study/results/finite_chain_map_N8.csv, all rows with V_over_omega >= 8 and delta_over_omega >= 3: density 0.4816-0.5278, gap_over_omega 0.0978-0.1646 (computed 27 Sept) -->. This is the finite-chain counterpart of the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ crystal. At weak coupling, large detuning overcomes the blockade: for $g=2$ and 2.5 the density rises above 0.75 just below $\Delta=2V_0$<!-- source: study/results/finite_chain_map_N8.csv; V = 2: density 0.735 at delta = 3.67, 0.786 at delta = 4.0; V = 2.5: density 0.746 at delta = 4.67, 0.794 at delta = 5.0. Detuning grid spacing 1/3, so the 0.75 crossings lie between 3.67 and 4.0 (2V0 = 4) and between 4.67 and 5.0 (2V0 = 5) (computed 27 Sept) -->, where the classical cost $2V_0-\Delta$ of adding an excitation between two excited neighbours vanishes. The order diagnostic $\langle Q^2\rangle$, with $Q=N^{-1}\sum_i(-1)^i(2n_i-1)$, never exceeds 0.237<!-- source: study/results/finite_chain_map_N8.csv, max staggered_spin_squared = 0.23683 at V_over_omega = 3, delta_over_omega = 1.333 (computed 27 Sept) -->, although either perfect alternating pattern has $Q^2=1$. The cause is the boundary: both end atoms of an even open chain favour excitation, which forces a domain wall, a 00 bond, into the chain. At $g=8$ and $\Delta/\Omega=3$, configurations with exactly one 00 bond and no 11 bond carry 76.7% of the ground-state weight, and the two perfect patterns carry 6.3%<!-- source: study/results/parity_summary.json, state_diagnostics entry N = 8, g = 8, delta = 3, cutoff = all, level 0: probability_exactly_one_00_and_no_11 = 0.7668, perfect_pattern_total_probability = 0.0635 -->. Section 3.3 analyses this wall.<!-- A single wall in N = 8 gives Q^2 = 0 (wall in the middle) or 1/4 (wall next to an end), which is why <Q^2> stays near 0.2. --> With one size and open boundaries, Fig. 6 is not a phase diagram.

**Selection of the crystal period.** Excitations closer than about $R_b$ are suppressed. At positive detunings of a few $\Omega$, too small to overcome this blockade, the densest allowed arrangement therefore has period $p$, the smallest integer larger than $R_b/a$. The uniform chain accordingly forms $\mathbb{Z}_2$, $\mathbb{Z}_3$ and $\mathbb{Z}_4$ crystals for $R_b/a$ roughly between 1 and 2, 2 and 3, and 3 and 4 [1,5,9]. The $\mathbb{Z}_2$ crystal melts through an Ising transition [6,7], the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ crystal through a three-state Potts point or chiral transitions [6,8,10], and floating phases separate the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ and $\mathbb{Z}_4$ regimes [9,11]. We therefore take one point inside each regime, $R_b/a=1.5$, 2.7 and 3.3 ($g=11.39$, 387.4 and 1291.5); preliminary ring calculations support this choice. At $R_b/a=1.5$, symmetry-resolved ED without basis restrictions places the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ melting point at $\Delta_c/\Omega=1.0785$ from 16- and 20-atom rings (1.0784 from 12 and 16 atoms), with a gap ratio (Sec. 2.5) of 7.96, close to the Ising value 8 [7]<!-- source: research/pilots/2026-09-27/outputs/crit_eps0.0_g11.39_d4.json: pair sizes [8, 10] (16 and 20 atoms): Dc = 1.07851, R_a = 7.955, R_b = 7.958; pair sizes [6, 8]: Dc = 1.07836. Zero-momentum, inversion-resolved periodic rings (scripts/pairk0.py, crit.py; brentq xtol 1e-4); d = 4 is the unrestricted pair basis; all pairs, minimum-image distance. Pilot; recompute in P3 -->. At $R_b/a=2.7$ and $\Delta/\Omega=5$, a 24-atom ring has three ground states degenerate to within $10^{-5}\,\Omega$, with pair-translation quantum numbers consistent with $1$ and $e^{\pm2\pi i/3}$, density 0.328 and the next level $2.79\,\Omega$ higher: the spectrum of a period-3 crystal<!-- source: research/pilots/2026-09-27/outputs/z3_24_0.0.txt (N = 24, dim = 103682, g = 387.4, D = 5.0, eps = 0.00): three levels dE = 0.00000 (printed to 1e-5) with printed T2 = +1.00, -0.50, -0.50; rho = 0.3279; fourth level dE = 2.79273. Pilot; recompute in P5 -->. That run excluded simultaneous excitation of adjacent atoms. By the second-order argument behind Eq. (3.1), these configurations would carry a total weight of only about $N\rho\,\Omega^2/2V_0^2\approx3\times10^{-5}$ (to be tested in P1)<!-- estimate, 27 Sept: each of the N*rho = 7.9 excitations has two ground-state neighbours; virtual excitation of one costs about V0 + V0/2^6 - Delta = 388 Omega with matrix element Omega/2, giving (1/2 / 388)^2 = 1.7e-6 per neighbour and 2.6e-5 in total. Not computed; P1 must check it -->. No uniform-ring spectrum at $R_b/a=3.3$ has been saved; all three reference points will be recomputed with the maintained package (P2, P3, P5; Fig. 7).
