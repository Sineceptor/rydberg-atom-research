### 3.3 How the physics varies with system size

A finite chain answers a bulk question only if the solver reaches converged sizes, the model keeps every relevant interaction, and the boundaries impose no state of their own.

#### 3.3.3 Parity and the boundary-forced domain wall

Figure 14 compares even and odd uniform chains at $g=8$ and $\Delta/\Omega=3$, inside the $\mathbb Z_2$ regime. Odd chains behave as small crystals, with $\langle Q^2\rangle\approx0.94$ and a gap of 2.27–$2.56\,\Omega$ for $N=5$–11<!-- source: study/results/parity_fixed_setting_scan.csv, cutoff = all: staggered_spin_squared = 0.9425, 0.9399, 0.9385, 0.9377 and gap = 2.5586, 2.4101, 2.3224, 2.2673 for N = 5, 7, 9, 11 -->. In even chains, between $N=4$ and 12, $\langle Q^2\rangle$ falls from 0.32 to 0.17 and the gap from 0.26 to $0.079\,\Omega$, and at $N=12$ the two perfect patterns carry only 2.2% of the ground state<!-- source: study/results/parity_fixed_setting_scan.csv, cutoff = all: staggered_spin_squared = 0.3185 (N = 4), 0.1729 (N = 12); gap = 0.2591 (N = 4), 0.0788 (N = 12); study/results/parity_summary.json, N = 12, cutoff = all, level 0: perfect_pattern_total_probability = 0.0222 -->.

The pseudo-spin picture explains the difference. The $B_m$ terms of Eq. (2.21) cancel in the bulk and leave end fields that favour $\tau=+1$ on the first pair and $\tau=-1$ on the last, that is, excitation of both end atoms, which is also seen in experiments [1, 2]. With a whole number of pairs no uniform $\tau$ satisfies both ends, so the ground state holds one wall from $\tau=+1$ to $\tau=-1$: a 00 bond between pairs (the opposite wall would be a blockaded 11 bond). The end fields outweigh the wall's ferromagnetic cost: excitations on opposite sides of the wall are $2ma+s$ rather than $2ma$ apart, so the wall lowers the classical energy (by $0.117\,\Omega$ for $N=8$, $g=8$, $\Delta/\Omega=3$ and $\Omega=0$)<!-- source: scratch check, 27 Sept (derived.py): N = 8, all pairs, Omega = 0, Delta = 3: E(10100101) - E(10101010) = -0.1170 (-0.1521 at eps = 0.3, g = 11.39, Delta = 2.5). With nearest neighbours only the difference vanishes: study/results/parity_summary.json, classical_interpretation (m + 1 degenerate maximum independent sets for even N = 2m) -->. The drive moves the wall by one pair with amplitude $t\approx h$ (Eq. 2.22), as for a kink in the ordered transverse-field Ising chain [3]. A chain of $P=N/2$ pairs thus holds a particle in a box of $P-1$ sites, with levels $E_q$ and wall-position probabilities $p_q(k)$ (wall between pairs $k-1$ and $k$)

$$E_q=E_{\rm w}-2t\cos\frac{q\pi}{P},\qquad q=1,\dots,P-1,\qquad(3.4)$$

$$p_q(k)=\frac2P\sin^2\frac{qk\pi}{P},\qquad k=1,\dots,P-1.\qquad(3.5)$$

Figures 15 and 16 support this picture. At $N=8$ the two lowest states, even and odd under reflection, place the 00 bond on the three bonds between pairs with probabilities 0.29, 0.42, 0.29 and 0.38, 0.03, 0.38<!-- source: study/results/parity_bond_probabilities.csv, N = 8, g = 8, Delta = 3, cutoff = all, left_atom = 1, 3, 5: level 0: 0.2859, 0.4163, 0.2859; level 1: 0.3760, 0.0260, 0.3760; reflection_expectation = +1 and -1 in parity_summary.json -->, against 0.25, 0.50, 0.25 and 0.50, 0, 0.50 from Eq. (3.5). One-wall configurations carry 76.7% of the ground state<!-- source: study/results/parity_summary.json, N = 8, g = 8, Delta = 3, cutoff = all, level 0: probability_exactly_one_00_and_no_11 = 0.7668 (0.7163 with nearest neighbours only, parity_low_states.csv) -->; the first excited state has 23% of its weight on the perfect patterns<!-- source: study/results/parity_summary.json, N = 8, g = 8, Delta = 3, cutoff = all, level 1: perfect_pattern_total_probability = 0.2314 -->, that is, on a wall at a chain end, which the hard-wall box omits. Setting the ED gap equal to $E_2-E_1$ from Eq. (3.4) at each size gives $t=0.094$–$0.108\,\Omega$ for the uniform chains of Fig. 14 ($N=6$–12) and $t=0.118$–$0.127\,\Omega$ for the dimerized chain of Table M1 ($N=16$–20, $\varepsilon=0.3$), within 30% of the leading-order field $h_0=0.133$ and $0.103\,\Omega$<!-- source: computed 27 Sept: t = gap / (2[cos(pi/P) - cos(2 pi/P)]), P = N/2, with gaps from study/results/parity_fixed_setting_scan.csv (uniform, N = 6, 8, 10, 12: t = 0.0942, 0.0968, 0.1023, 0.1076) and validation/results/scaling_benchmark.json ed[*].gap (N = 16, 18, 20: t = 0.1181, 0.1230, 0.1273); h0 = (1/4)[1/Delta + 1/(V_s - Delta)] from Eq. (2.22) with V_s = g/(1-eps)^6: 0.1333 (g = 8, eps = 0, Delta = 3) and 0.1027 (g = 11.39, eps = 0.3, Delta = 2.5) -->.

In an ordered phase, therefore, an even open chain measures the wall band. Its gap closes faster than $1/N$ ($N(E_1-E_0)$ falls from 1.10 to 0.95 between $N=8$ and 12<!-- source: study/results/parity_fixed_setting_scan.csv, cutoff = all: 8 x 0.1369 = 1.095, 12 x 0.0788 = 0.946; likewise 16 x 0.0512 = 0.819 and 20 x 0.0362 = 0.723 from scaling_benchmark.json (computed 27 Sept) -->) and can be mistaken for the tunnelling splitting of two crystals, while order parameters average over wall positions. For the dimerized chain, extrapolating Eq. (3.4) gives wall level spacings of about $6\times10^{-3}\,\Omega$ at $N=50$ and $4\times10^{-4}\,\Omega$ at $N=200$, far below the energy spread $\sigma_E\approx0.02\,\Omega$ of the 200-atom DMRG state<!-- source: computed 27 Sept with t = 0.118-0.127 in Eq. An extrapolation, not a calculation -->. This soft mode is the likely reason DMRG converges slowly. For a wall spread over the chain, Eq. (3.5), the mean $\lvert\langle\tau_k\rangle\rvert$ over the central half is independent of $N$; it is 0.45 at $N=20$ (converged) and 0.42 at $N=50$, but 0.53 and 0.79 at $N=100$ and 200, which suggests that the wall is still localized, as in the initial state<!-- source: validation/results/scaling_benchmark.json, dmrg_pair: bulk_abs_pair_order = 0.453 (N = 20, mmax = null and 8, converged), 0.420 (N = 50), 0.526 (N = 100), 0.789 (N = 200) (mmax = 8); definition in validation/scaling_benchmark.py: mean of |<n_L - n_R>| over pairs P//4 ... Bulk properties must therefore come from rings, from odd chains one atom longer than a multiple of the crystal period (as in the 13- and 51-atom experiments of Ref. #### 3.3.4 Confirmation at large N (planned)

Computations P6–P8 will test these conclusions at large $N$ (Fig. 17). P6 repairs infinite DMRG, which must first reproduce the pilot ring-ED energy of $-1.2284\,\Omega$ per atom at $\varepsilon=0.55$, $\Delta/\Omega=2.5$ and $g=11.39$<!-- source: docs/RESEARCH_PLAN.md, P6 row; docs/DIMERIZED_THEORY.md Sec. 6 (pilot ring-ED value, preliminary) -->. P7 removes the wall with odd chains on atom sites or with end atoms pinned by a local detuning (`local_delta`); an odd number of pairs is not enough, because it removes only the wall of the proposed pair-density wave, not the $\tau$ wall of the $\tau$-ordered crystals. P7 then raises $\chi$ and the sweep limit until the variance and the central-half order converge. These runs also give the central charge of Fig. 12; open-chain fits of Eq. (2.18) [4] must include the alternating boundary term [5]. P8 extends the ED wall analysis to $N=20$ and tests Eqs. (3.4)–(3.5) directly.

These conclusions rest on ED of uniform chains of at most 12 atoms ($g\le16$) and dimerized chains of at most 20 atoms, on a hard-wall box model matched to one gap at each size, and on DMRG runs of 50 atoms or more that have not met the convergence criteria. Until P6–P8 are complete, statements about long dimerized chains rely on the preliminary ring calculations of Sec. 3.2.
